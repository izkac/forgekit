Recalibrate triage and review

Operator brief — change recalibrate-triage-and-review

TL;DR

Forge currently can't let anything through. The rule that decides "this is too small for Forge" requires three things to be true at once that can't all be true, so in 87 recorded sessions nothing has ever been skipped. Separately, the strictest setting reviews nearly three times as often as the middle one and finds the same number of problems per unit of work.

This change makes the skip rule reachable, moves the real decision to a point where Forge actually knows how big the job is, and re-prices reviews so the money goes where it pays.

What's wrong today

The escape hatch is welded shut. To skip Forge, work must be a pure question and a typo fix and you must have typed /forge:skip. Those can't all be true. The same page then contradicts itself, saying that even when the answer is "no, this doesn't need Forge", you still need to opt out manually.

Extra reviews aren't finding extra problems. Measured from Forge's own records across 87 sessions:

SettingTasksReviews per taskProblems found per 100 tasks
brisk440.070.0
standard1870.134.8
thorough7170.364.7

Thorough spends 2.8x the reviews and finds the same amount. Two honest caveats: "problems found" is a rough stand-in for review value, and the sample is small enough that this shows no visible return rather than proving there is none. It's enough to act on, and it's easy to undo.

The dial only turns one way. How much ceremony to spend is decided from the first word of your message — before Forge knows how many files, how many tasks, or what risk is involved. Nothing ever revisits it. There's a rule that makes sessions stricter when they turn out large; there's no rule anywhere that makes them lighter when they turn out small.

What you'll get

  • Small work can actually leave Forge — and when it does, it's recorded, so "how often does this happen" becomes a number instead of a guess.
  • A checkpoint after brainstorming: if the shaped-up job turns out to be two tasks in one file, Forge offers to step aside rather than write a proposal, design, task list, wiring matrix and brief for it.
  • Reviews re-priced — see the table below.
  • The ceremony dial turns both ways.
  • forge analyze gains the review-yield table, so you can check this change actually worked instead of trusting it.

thoroughyes noyesyesfull / 3

Two deliberate reversals. Thorough stops reviewing after every single task — it keeps its meaning through deeper reviews and more fix rounds, not more of them. And the lightest settings gain the end-of-change review, which today they skip. One reviewer reading the whole change is the cheapest useful check there is; several reviewers each reading a slice is the expensive one that isn't paying.

Nothing changes for risky work. Anything touching money, login, shared contracts, migrations or secrets still gets reviewed immediately after every single task, at every setting, exactly as today. That floor is the one review rule with solid evidence behind it, and it isn't part of this change.

How the decision moves

flowchart TD
  P["Your message"] --> T{"Obviously trivial?"}
  T -->|yes| S1["Skip Forge"]
  T -->|no| B["Brainstorm — shape the work"]
  B --> C{"Small, one area,
nothing wired, not risky?"}
  C -->|yes| S2["Offer to step aside
(recorded either way)"]
  C -->|no| PL["Write the tracked change"]
  PL --> D{"Plan-time facts"}
  D --> E["Pace can go up
or down"]

The first check stays cheap and stays biased toward doing the work properly. The decision that matters moves to after brainstorming, because that's the first moment Forge knows what the job actually is.

Risks and open questions

  • We can measure waste, not blind spots. "Problems found" goes down if reviews were pointless — but a problem nobody looked for also produces nothing. So this guardrail catches over-spending, not under-spending. Testing the other direction needs bugs traced back to the session that introduced them; noted as a follow-up, not solved here.
  • The scoring rules have to move at the same time. The scorecard currently expects sessions to only ever get stricter, so a session that correctly gets lighter would be marked down. Fixed in the same change, not after.
  • Other machines won't see the doc changes until forgekit install --skills forge --force is run there.
  • The evidence mixes three projects and two earlier versions of the settings table. The direction is clear; the exact numbers aren't a formal result.

Out of scope

  • Changing the high-risk review floor.
  • Deleting the thorough setting — it keeps a narrower meaning.
  • Tracing later bugs back to the session that caused them (the follow-up above).
  • Anything to do with the campaign benchmark, which is a separate change.
