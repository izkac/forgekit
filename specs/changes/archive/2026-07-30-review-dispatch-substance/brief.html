A review has to actually happen

Operator brief — change review-dispatch-substance

Forge refuses to finish a high-risk change unless someone other than the coordinator reviewed it. Right now that check can be satisfied by a reviewer that does nothing: start a helper, have it exit immediately, and Forge counts it as a real review.

After this change, a reviewer that did no work no longer counts as evidence — Forge falls back to reading the review write-up instead, and a write-up that admits nobody reviewed the change gets refused at the gate, which is what should have happened all along.

What you'll get

  • A helper that starts and stops without doing anything can no longer vouch for a review.
  • Nothing is refused that isn't refused today — when the check can't be satisfied, Forge falls back to reading the review write-up, exactly as it already does when there's no record at all.
  • A real reviewer's work is unaffected. Measured against every review helper ever run on this machine, the quietest did roughly three times more work than the new bar.
  • The safety check that guards the finish gate gets its own tests. It didn't have any, despite deciding whether high-risk work can be marked done.

How it works

Forge already records how much work each helper did — how many round-trips it made with the model — and has been storing that number without ever looking at it. This change looks at it. If the busiest helper for a review made fewer than five round-trips, Forge treats the record as unhelpful rather than as proof, and reads the written review instead.

flowchart TD
  A["Finish a high-risk change"] --> B{"Did a helper review it?"}
  B -- "no record" --> P["Read the written review"]
  B -- "yes" --> C{"Did it do enough work?"}
  C -- "fewer than 5 round-trips" --> P
  C -- "5 or more" --> D["Counts as an independent review"]
  P --> E{"Does the write-up claim
an outside reviewer?"}
  E -- "no" --> F["Refused — review it properly"]
  E -- "yes" --> D
  D --> G["Finish allowed"]

Two details matter more than they look:

  • One real reviewer, not many small ones. Forge measures the busiest single helper, not the total across all of them. Otherwise ten do-nothing helpers add up and clear the bar together.
  • A helper you cancelled doesn't vouch for one you didn't. If you stop a long-running reviewer and a do-nothing one runs beside it, the cancelled one's effort is not counted toward the other.

The bar is five: comfortably under every real reviewer, comfortably over the fake. The plan records that this has to be re-measured before anyone moves it — a threshold set without checking real data is how an earlier version of this check had to be reverted.

What changes for you

No new commands, and nothing to configure. The only difference you might notice: if a review helper is cancelled early or its logs have been cleaned up, Forge will fall back to reading the written review rather than trusting the record. That can cost a session a couple of points on its scorecard; it cannot block you.

Risks and open questions

  • More weight lands on reading the write-up. That path has known weak spots, already recorded as open findings. This change makes them busier without making them worse. The real fix — having Forge itself mark the review file when it dispatches a reviewer — is a separate, larger piece of work and is not started here.
  • Someone who reads this brief could pad a fake reviewer past five round-trips. True. This is not claimed to be airtight — it removes the one-line fake and makes cheating cost real work. Closing it properly is the marking change above.
  • Deliberately not fixed: a separate hole where a session tied to two coding sessions, one of them unreadable, can still get a confident wrong answer. Different module, still on the list.

Out of scope

  • The finish gate's own rule is unchanged — it still asks only whether the final review was independent.
  • Sessions already finished are not re-judged. Their verdicts were frozen on purpose.
  • No change to how the written review is interpreted.
